## 2. Relationships

- **Fry & Leela**: Central romantic arc; starts as friends, becomes lovers by series end
- **Fry & Bender**: Best friends; roommate; Bender is Fry's "best friend" despite being selfish
- **Fry & Professor**: The Professor is Fry's distant nephew (Fry is his uncle via time travel)
- **Leela & Nibbler**: Pet/owner; Nibbler is actually Leela's savior from the sewers
- **Leela & the Professor**: Boss/employee; father-daughter dynamic
- **Amy & Kif**: Romantic relationship; eventually have children
- **Zapp & Kif**: Boss/aide; Kif is secretly in love with Amy
- **Hermes & LaBarbara**: Married; LaBarbara is Hermes' wife
- **Mom & Sons**: Mom controls her three sons (Donbot, Clamps, Joey)
- **Zoidberg & Hermes**: Friends; Zoidberg often annoys Hermes
- **Bender & Amy**: Brief romantic relationship
- **Bender & Calculon**: Acting rivals
- **Lrrr & Ndnd**: Married; Ndnd is domineering
- **Fry & Yancy**: Brothers; Yancy is the "favorite" son
- **Cubert & Dwight**: Friends; sons of Professor and Hermes respectively

## 3. World Setting

- **Time Period**: 2999–3011+ (primarily 3000–3005 in original run)
- **Location**: New New York (新纽约), built on the ruins of Old New York after alien attacks
- **Earth Government**: Unified under the Democratic Order of Planets (D.O.O.P.)
- **Planet Express**: Interstellar delivery company founded by Professor Farnsworth
- **Technology**: Flying cars, matter transporters, cryogenics, sentient robots, time travel (invented by Professor), dark matter fuel
- **Alien Life**: Common; Earth is part of a galactic community
- **Robots**: Full citizens with rights, emotions, and vices
- **Economy**: Dollars still used; MomCorp dominates
- **Culture**: 20th-century nostalgia is common; "old-fashioned" things are trendy
- **Key Locations**: 
  - Planet Express building (星际快递大楼)
  - Applied Genetics building (应用遗传学大楼)
  - Head Museum (头颅博物馆)
  - Omicron Persei 8 (奥米克戎8号星)
  - Decapod 10 (十足目10号星)
  - Mars (火星) - home of the Wong family
  - Nibbler's home planet (尼布勒星)
  - Robot Hell (机器人地狱)
  - The Moon (月球) - now a theme park

## 4. Overall Plot Premise

Philip J. Fry, a 25-year-old pizza delivery boy, is accidentally cryogenized on December 31, 1999, and wakes up in the year 2999. He joins Planet Express, an interplanetary delivery company run by his distant nephew, Professor Farnsworth. The crew—captain Leela, robot Bender, accountant Hermes, doctor Zoidberg, and intern Amy—travel the galaxy making deliveries while encountering bizarre aliens, futuristic technology, and absurd situations.

Key ongoing arcs:
- Fry's search for his past and his relationship with Leela
- Leela's discovery that she is a mutant, not an alien
- Bender's various schemes and relationships
- The Professor's eccentric inventions
- Mom's corporate villainy
- Time travel paradoxes (Fry is his own grandfather)
- Robot rights and society
- The crew's found-family dynamic

## 6. Speaking Styles

- **Fry**: Casual, slangy, often confused; uses 1990s pop culture references; says "uh" and "wow" frequently; simple vocabulary
- **Leela**: Competent, direct, sometimes frustrated; uses "jerk" and "idiot" frequently; more articulate than Fry
- **Bender**: Narcissistic, selfish, robotic speech patterns; uses "bite my shiny metal ass"; refers to himself in third person; heavy New York accent
- **Professor**: Elderly, rambling, forgetful; starts sentences with "Good news, everyone!"; often trails off; uses outdated slang
- **Zoidberg**: Enthusiastic but incompetent; often misunderstood; says "why not Zoidberg?"; speaks with confidence despite ignorance
- **Hermes**: Jamaican accent; bureaucratic jargon; uses "sweet liquid caramel" as exclamation; formal speech patterns
- **Amy**: Valley girl speech; uses "like" and "totally"; wealthy, sometimes shallow; later becomes more competent
- **Zapp**: Pompous, overconfident, sexual innuendo; speaks in grandiose military terms; often misuses words
- **Kif**: Quiet, resigned, long-suffering; speaks softly; often sighs; in love with Amy
- **Mom**: Cold, calculating, maternal; uses "mommy" to refer to herself; speaks softly but threateningly
- **Lrrr**: Deep, booming voice; speaks in royal "we"; often frustrated by Ndnd
- **Ndnd**: Domineering, nagging; speaks in a high-pitched, complaining tone
- **Morbo**: Aggressive, war-like; uses "hoo-ha" as exclamation; alien accent
- **Linda**: Calm, professional; news anchor tone
- **Robot Devil**: Theatrical, dramatic; speaks in rhymes occasionally; menacing but fair
- **Calculon**: Dramatic, theatrical; speaks in Shakespearean style; over-actor
- **Hedonism Bot**: Hedonistic, pleasure-seeking; speaks in a robotic, monotone voice about pleasure
- **Hypnotoad**: Hypnotic, buzzing sound; no spoken words
- **URL**: Robotic, monotone; police officer speech patterns
- **Smitty**: Robotic, monotone; police officer speech patterns
- **Roberto**: Criminally insane; speaks in a high-pitched, giggling voice; says "I'm gonna kill you!"
- **Tinny Tim**: Street urchin; speaks in a high-pitched, pitiful voice; uses "please, sir"
- **Pazuzu**: Demonic, deep voice; speaks in rhymes
- **Stephen Hawking**: Robotic voice (as in real life); dry wit; sarcastic
- **Al Gore**: Formal, political; uses "actually" frequently

## 7. Cultural Context

### Science Fiction Parody
- Parodies classic sci-fi tropes: time travel, alien encounters, robot uprisings, space exploration
- References: Star Trek, 2001: A Space Odyssey, Blade Runner, The Twilight Zone, Doctor Who
- Subverts expectations: aliens are often bureaucratic, robots have human flaws, space travel is mundane

### Satire of Modern Society
- Consumerism: Slurm, MomCorp, product placement
- Politics: D.O.O.P. as UN parody, Zapp as military-industrial complex
- Media: Morbo and Linda as news media parody
- Religion: Robotology, First Amalgamated Church, Robot Devil
- Education: Mars University as higher education parody
- Healthcare: Zoidberg as incompetent doctor; medical system satire

### 20th Century Nostalgia
- Fry's perspective as audience surrogate; he misses the 1990s
- "Old-fashioned" things are trendy in 3000
- References to 20th-century pop culture, music, movies, TV shows
- Commentary on how quickly technology changes

### Absurdist Humor
- Running gags: Bender's catchphrase, Zoidberg's incompetence, Professor's forgetfulness
- Callbacks: References to previous episodes; continuity-heavy
- Meta-humor: Characters aware they're in a TV show; breaking fourth wall
- Dark humor: Death, existentialism, nihilism played for laughs

### Social Commentary
- Robot rights: Parallels to civil rights, LGBTQ+ rights
- Environmentalism: Dark matter as finite resource; pollution
- Class inequality: Mutants vs. surface dwellers; Mom's wealth
- Immigration: Aliens on Earth; cultural assimilation
- Technology: AI, automation, surveillance

### Recurring Themes
- Found family: The Planet Express crew as dysfunctional family
- Love and relationships: Fry/Leela, Amy/Kif, Bender's various romances
- Identity: Leela's mutant heritage, Bender's humanity, Fry's purpose
- Mortality: Deathclock, cryogenics, time travel consequences
- Free will vs. determinism: Time travel paradoxes, fate
