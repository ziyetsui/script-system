## S11E01

**Futurama — S11E01**

**Plot Summary**

The Planet Express crew delivers a gravity pump to Coney Island Community College, Fry's alma mater. While wandering the campus's midway, Fry discovers a museum exhibit about the 1999 hotdog-eating contest where he was humiliated by the legendary champion Brocky J. Fry learns that cows are now extinct, so modern hotdogs are plant-based and lack the slippery "coefficient of friction" of real beef dogs, making them impossible to eat competitively.

Determined to revive the sport, the Professor attempts to clone a cow from DNA extracted via mosquito-in-amber (a Jurassic Park reference). The first attempt fails—he clones mosquito cells instead. Fry then suggests using an undercooked hotdog from the museum's Hall of Fame display. The cloning succeeds, but the hotdog had Brocky J's saliva on it, resulting in a human-cow hybrid creature with Brocky J's memories (gleaned from his audiobook autobiography).

Rather than slaughter the suffering hybrid, the Professor pivots to lab-grown meat, creating "FarnDogs"—100% meat-based veggie dogs grown from muscle fiber. The hotdog-eating contest is revived, sponsored by FarnDog Industries. Fry trains poorly, gaining 15 pounds. The cloned Brocky J enters the contest and taunts Fry, referencing his autobiography's details about Fry's mother.

During the contest, Fry eats 82 hotdogs and is in severe pain. The cloned Brocky J, having never eaten a hotdog before and finding the experience unbearable, begs Fry to eat him alive to end his suffering. Fry reluctantly does so, consuming the hybrid creature. With the "dozy" of Brocky J himself, Fry wins with a record 163 hotdogs, claiming the Beef Belt.

**Important Character Actions**

- Fry revisits his competitive-eating past and commits to winning the rematch against Brocky J's legacy
- Professor Farnsworth clones a human-cow hybrid from contaminated hotdog DNA, then invents lab-grown meat
- Leela supports Fry's training, finding his "dad bod" attractive
- The cloned Brocky J trash-talks Fry, reads his autobiography via audiobook, and ultimately begs to be consumed
- Zoidberg fails to help with Jamaican citronella
- Hermes briefly catches Fry changing on the street

**Newly Established Names/Terms**

- **FarnDogs**: Lab-grown meat-based veggie dogs; Professor Farnsworth's commercial product
- **Beef Belt**: The championship belt awarded at the hotdog-eating contest
- **Wienerdome**: The venue for the revived Coney Island Hotdog Eating Contest
- **"Nice Try" Fry**: The announcer's nickname for Fry at the contest
- **Dr. Carny**: The recipient of the gravity pump at Coney Island Community College
- **Don Cunningham**: The sportscaster covering the contest (in-person at the Wienerdome)

**Continuity-Relevant Facts**

- Cows are confirmed extinct in this era; hotdogs are plant-based
- Fry's 1999 loss to Brocky J and his subsequent cryogenic freezing are central to the plot
- Brocky J died 1,000 years ago from eating an undercooked hotdog
- Fry's mother is referenced in Brocky J's autobiography (Brocky stole her phone number after the '99 contest)
- Fry's "no-chew" swallowing technique and stretchy stomach are established skills
- The Professor admits Planet Express had gone out of business temporarily

**Jokes and References**

- Jurassic Park DNA-extraction method (via mosquito in amber)
- The wienermobile predates the hotdog; Model W was Henry Ford's mass-produced wienermobile
- Frankfurt/Hamburg hotdog origin irony (similar to real-world etymology joke)

- Protesters with blank signs unsure what they're protesting (lab-grown meat)
- Bender's "mis-reckoned" / "yee-haw" Texas vs. Brazil gag

- Fry: "I love 100% all-beef hotdogs more than anything in the world! Well, anything except Leela"

**Unresolved Threads**

- The Professor's bank account ("I-I mean, our bank account") suggests possible financial motives
- Bender's final line "Not for long!" hints at a tag or post-credits scene not fully resolved in the subtitles

## S11E02

# Futurama S11E02 Summary

## Episode Plot

Bender takes Fry to visit his grandmother, Abuelatron, at a retirement home. She reveals she was scammed by her boyfriend Sebastian, who convinced her to give him the fortune from selling her Mexican tortilla business. Determined to get revenge, Bender goes undercover as an elderly robot at the home, complete with metal fatigue and a "Jitterphone" (a device that attracts scammers). However, Bender himself falls victim to a scammer named Daniella, who pitches him a catfish farm investment. He falls in love and hands over his bank details.

The crew devises a counter-scam: they'll fake a ship impoundment to trick the scammer into paying a $1,000 fee using fake "Planet Express Fun Bucks." The plan gets a boost when Sheriff Burl Hooten of Scamton County actually pulls them over and impounds the ship for real. Idabelle Sue (Daniella's real name) then pulls her own scam, swapping the bank's keypad so the decimal point is actually a tiny zero—turning a $1,000 withdrawal into $1,000,000. The crew gets arrested for dancing (a crime in Scamton), posts bail, and escapes. Bender learns the million came from his own grandmother's account. He proposes to Idabelle, but she rejects him, saying she's fallen in love with someone else.

Bender returns to Abuelatron expecting to apologize for losing her money, only to discover she's now wealthy—the catfish farm she invested in became wildly successful after the fish ate "nutritious cash flakes" and bred prolifically. She's also acquired a sexy butlerbot. Bender, rejected by Idabelle, declares he'll redirect his love to his grandmother.

- **Abuelatron**: Reveals she was scammed, later revealed to be wealthy from catfish farm stock

## S11E03

# Futurama S11E03 — Episode Summary

## Plot

DOOP President Glab, in her final term, attempts to engineer a lifetime reign by packing the Assembly with stooge dictators and admitting the Vampire Empire, whose tie-breaking vote would let her suspend term limits. Her tool is a gemjewelium-crystal grand piano "gifted" to the Vampires — actually an improvised weapon of mass destruction (2,000 megasproings of string tension; one wrong chord would implode it and compress the crystals to hypercritical mass).

Admirella, working secretly with pirates Long John Longjohns (Governor of the Pirate Republic) and Don Electro, plus Lieutenant Kif Kroker (as inside man on Zapp's Nimbus), stages a pirate raid to steal the piano and expose Glab. The Planet Express crew — Leela, Amy, Professor, Zoidberg — follow a faint gemjewelium radiation trail, infiltrate the Pirate Republic by posing as buccaneers, and find Fry, Bender, and Kif alive. The combined group arrives at the DOOP Assembly just as Glab forces the vote.

The Vampire Empire's membership requires their anthem be played first. Bender sits at the piano and plays "Whole Lot of Shakin' Goin' On," shaking Glab until she resigns and nominates Admirella as her permanent successor. The piano implodes — but Pirates had already swapped the real gemjewelium for cubic zirconium, so no nuclear detonation. The Vampire Empire is rejected; the Pirate Planet is admitted.

## Character actions

- Zapp Brannigan — Delivers the rigged piano; calls Fry and Bender his "civilian know-nothings." Refuses to surrender, rams the pirates, is marooned on a derelict with a lifetime of snacks. Spends the rest of the episode stuck there.
- Kif Kroker — Secretly Admirella's co-conspirator. Sabotages the Nimbus by opening the airlock instead of raising shields, letting the pirates board. Confesses to Leela and is forgiven by Amy ("I'm sure it took a lot of hard work, dear").
- Fry — Captured aboard the pirate ship; dines, bowls, and attends the officers' disco. Befriends Buddy the parrot, who imitates everyone around him.
- Bender — Plays Rachmaninoff (off-screen joke) and later "Whole Lotta Shakin' Goin' On" on the rigged piano to blackmail Glab. "I thought you'd never ask" catchphrase recurs twice.
- Leela — Leads the rescue; infiltrates as "Pirate Queen of the bad ship, Planet Express Ship." Initially suspects Kif of real treachery.
- Amy — Poses as "Merciless Amy Wong"; bribes the Pirate Republic with a $500,000 Starbucks gift card; defends Kif ("Kif's no traitor! He's so honest, he withholds payroll taxes from our kids' allowance.") — confirming she and Kif are married with children.

- Zoidberg — Sent in first as "expendable"; enjoys the warm turtle soup on the derelict.
- Hermes — Briefly listed as missing; no speaking role established.

## S11E04

# Futurama S11E04 — Wheel World

## Plot

The Professor walks in trailing a flooded ceiling and can't remember his own catchphrase announcement. He's forgotten to pick up Cubert from Chess Game Reenactors Club. The crew's medic (implied) diagnoses him with Absentminded Scientist Syndrome (A.S.S.) — the worst case he's ever seen. The Professor recalls that, roughly a century ago, he set up a memory-loss research lab on the small, animal-rights-activist-unknown planetoid Vivisectoid Alpha, where lab rats were confined to a difficult maze in hopes their brains would evolve enhanced chemistry. A year was supposed to be enough; it has been one hundred.

The Planet Express ship arrives to find Vivisectoid Alpha missing from sensors. They crash into empty space, blowing a critical vacuum tube and stranding themselves. Leela notices they've landed on a colossal ring-shaped world whose rotation simulates gravity. The Professor names it "Wheel World." In the Forbidden Zone they meet Dr. Vermius of Ratcliffe University, who is collecting "relics" that are, in fact, fragments of the Professor's old lab equipment and a pair of his lecturing teeth. The Professor's century-old maze rats have evolved into a large, civilized, ring-dwelling society, with bigger brains demanding bigger bodies.

Vermius ushers the crew — wearing rat-prosthetic disguises — to Ratcliffe University in the western semicircle, the finest research institute in their world. He introduces his colleague Dr. Pestia, a flea researcher whose work is being picketed by "Free the fleas!" zealots (whom Vermius calls "flea-huggers"). The University archive's sacred book recounts that rats were once imprisoned in a maze by the Evil One, until a "cheese apocalypse" blew the maze apart and left a glittering ring. Vermius's life work is to identify the Evil One so rats can exact revenge. Leela calls the Professor out for being suspiciously familiar with the relics; he deflects.

That night, the Professor privately recovers his old Orbiting Security Drone 139-B and plays its recording: the cheese cavern beneath Vivisectoid Alpha built up pressure, exploded, melted the planet's rocky ring into the solid surface the rats now live on, and blew the maze apart. The rats didn't build the ring — they merely infested it. Vermius notices the Professor doodled the Evil One's torture devices and confronts him: the Professor is the Evil One.

Vermius, Pestia, and the rat university imprison the crew and reverse the role — running them through "experiments" (maze tests, makeup trials) while a "humonic plague" sweeps the rat population. Bender is thrilled by the maze and electrocution. The Professor volunteers to stay behind as a lab animal so Pestia can test plague cures on him, and the rats let the rest of the crew leave with a working replacement vacuum tube. Fry is dropped over the ring's edge as the crew escapes; Bender insists on coming back for him.

Trapped on the ring's underside, the crew is cornered by the rat patrol, but their running around the ring speeds up its rotation and increases gravity. Pestia (apparently infected herself) discovers fleas are the plague's vector — and Fry's slathered-on test makeup shielded him: the arsenic in the cosmetics leached into his bloodstream and killed the bacteria. Bender's "hot, toxic lips" (the contaminated makeup) are used to inoculate the rats, curing the plague. The Professor reports that the maze ordeal actually restored his memory to tip-top shape, so he no longer needs their brain juice. The rats decide to forgive the Evil One with "a fond smile" and hint at a future visit. The crew departs with the repaired vacuum tube.

## Important Character Actions

- Professor Farnsworth: forgets his own "good news" announcement, ignores Cubert, ignores a flooding ceiling, and is later revealed to be the rats' messianic "Evil One." He recovers Drone 139-B, watches the planet's destruction, and offers himself as a permanent lab animal before ultimately leaving with his memory restored.
- Leela: takes charge during the crash, deduces the ring-world physics, devises the "jump jerks" escape plan, and lands a sharp "I'm not the Evil One" retort.
- Bender: bounces on the ring's gravity like a beach ball, falls in love during his stay, voluntarily electrocutes himself on the maze's shock button, and donates his contaminated lips to cure the plague.
- Fry: gets a thick layer of test makeup applied ("a thousand poison needles"), which inadvertently shields and cures him, then accidentally cures the entire rat species.
- Hermes: spots the clue in the Professor's doodle and publicly names him the Evil One.
- Zoidberg: offended at being compared to infesting rats; gets a self-promoting shoutout.
- Cubert Farnsworth: forgotten at Chess Game Reenactors Club; his line "I got checkmated really hard today" opens the episode.
- Dr. Vermius: rat archaeologist, host at Ratcliffe, life-long hunter of the Evil One's identity.
- Dr. Pestia: rat colleague, flea researcher, eventual plague-cure discoverer; criticized by "flea-hugger" activists.

## S11E05

Plot Summary

Amy's birthday coincides with the announcement of teen pop sensation Coco Charm's "Revolution Tour." Kif scrapes together his savings to buy tickets, but Ticketmistress inflates the price with a $500 "convenience fee" (her only affordable seats are in the back row, where sound arrives after the song ends). Amy reveals she already had VIP tickets and gives them to Fry and Leela as birthday gifts for Leela; the rest of the crew scrambles for last-minute seats. At Madison Cube Garden, opening act DJ TimeFilla performs "Airline Customer Service Hold Music" before Coco Charm takes the stage and delivers a set of breakup-pop songs.

After the show, Coco announces her encore: declaring that music is "computer code for the mind," she uses her melodies to reprogram the audience, turning her fans ("the Charmy") into a coordinated army ("the Charmandos"). She orders them to destroy Earth's stockpile of doomsday weapons to bring "peace on Earth," leaving Earth defenseless. Richard Nixon (Earth's president) orders four-skirt General Zapp Brannigan to attack Coco's giant floating cube-stadium, but Zapp's weapons have already been dismantled. He is accompanied by Ensign Zig, daughter of Grobel Zig, whom Zapp previously got killed.

The Planet Express crew attempts to fly through a T-shirt-vendor access trench to transmit a "surrender counter-melody" via Bluetooth to Coco's stadium. Fry, however, gets the tune stuck in his head and begins compulsively dancing. Zoidberg performs "raw-claw" brain surgery to excise the affected neurons; Fry briefly hums Coco's melody afterward, causing Earth to surrender instantly.

Coco publicly disarms Earth. With no weapons, Lrrr of Omicron Persei 8 invades Earth unopposed, but immediately recognizes Coco as a fan and asks for an autograph on his skull. Spared, Lrrr abandons the invasion, citing Coco's song "I'm Gonna Be the Bigger Girl." Left without a target, Coco's army looks for someone else to fight; the entire universe unites against Ticketmistress. The Charmandos destroy Ticketmistress's servers, only to learn Mom runs the company — though she claims every convenience-fee cent goes to the Department of Health and Human and Nonhuman Services to fund schools, hospitals, and childhood hunger (Kif needed it for his tail-removal surgery). Once the servers are wrecked, Mom immediately announces she will now keep all the fees herself.

Kif confesses to Amy that he stole the ticket money from her; Amy forgives him. Bender reminds everyone that Fry is dead. The episode ends with the Charmy celebrating, calling for DJ TimeFilla's return.
