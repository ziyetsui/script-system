- **First air**: December 17, 1989 on Fox Broadcasting Company
- **Seasons/Episodes**: 37+ seasons, 809+ episodes (ongoing)
- **Genre**: Animated sitcom, satire, surreal humor
- **Creator**: Matt Groening; developed by Groening, James L. Brooks, Sam Simon

## Relationships
- **Core family**: Homer & Marge (married); Bart, Lisa, Maggie (children)
- **Extended**: Grampa Abe (Homer's father); Patty & Selma (Marge's sisters); Jacqueline Bouvier (Marge's mother)
- **Work**: Homer ↔ Burns/Smithers (plant); Moe (bar regular); Lenny/Carl (coworkers)
- **School**: Bart & Lisa ↔ Skinner, Mrs. Krabappel (teacher), Nelson (bully), Milhouse (best friend), Ralph Wiggum
- **Neighborhood**: Flanders (devout neighbor); Dr. Hibbert (doctor); Comic Book Guy; various recurring townsfolk

## World Setting
- **Location**: Springfield — fictional "Middle America" town in an unspecified U.S. state (intentionally ambiguous; geography shifts per episode: coastline, desert, mountains, farmland as jokes require)
- **Key landmarks**: Springfield Nuclear Power Plant (Sector 7-G), Springfield Elementary, Moe's Tavern, Kwik-E-Mart, City Hall, Android's Dungeon (comic shop), Evergreen Terrace (Simpsons' address: 742)
- **Floating timeline**: Characters do not age; episodes generally set in production year; flashbacks/forwards float relative to air date
- **Tone**: Satirical exaggeration of American middle-class life, institutions, media, politics, religion, pop culture

## Overall Plot Premise
A dysfunctional but loving working-class family navigates absurd daily life in a surreal Springfield. Homer's incompetence at the nuclear plant, Bart's school pranks, Lisa's intellectual/activist pursuits, Marge's domestic mediation, and Maggie's silent observation anchor episodic stories that parody sitcom tropes, current events, and cultural touchstones. Continuity is loose; status quo resets each episode.

## Cultural Context for Translators
- **Satire targets**: American suburbia, nuclear family, corporate greed (Burns), political corruption (Mayor Quimby), religious hypocrisy (Lovejoy), media stupidity (Krusty, Kent Brockman), education failure (Skinner/Krabappel), celebrity culture
- **Meta-humor**: Frequent Fox-bashing ("Fox sucks!"), self-referential jokes about animation budget, writers' room, cancellation fears
- **Cutaway gags**: Chalkboard lines (Bart's detention writing), couch gags (opening variants), Lisa's saxophone solos — often untranslated or localized
- **Treehouse of Horror**: Non-canon Halloween anthology; parody horror/sci-fi tropes; translation should signal genre shift
- **Celebrity cameos**: Hundreds of guest stars as themselves or characters; preserve name recognition
- **Running gags**: Hans Moleman's deaths, Sideshow Bob's revenge, Gil's failures, Dr. Nick's malpractice, Hans Moleman, Crazy Cat Lady, Yes Guy, Blue-Haired Lawyer
- **Liberal/progressive bent**: Writers acknowledge left-leaning satire; government/corporations portrayed as corrupt; religion mocked but family prays in crises
- **Global impact**: "D'oh!" in OED; "meh", "cromulent", "embiggen" entered dictionaries; influenced adult animation boom (South Park, Family Guy, Futurama)
- **Censorship/localization notes**: Arab version replaces beer→soda, hot dogs→Egyptian sausages; Taiwan dub inserts local news/celebrity jokes; HK Disney+ removed Tiananmen/forced-labor episodes
